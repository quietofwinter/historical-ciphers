=== Letter 1. 22 August 1656. Signed Jo. Waddall. (Birch vol. 5, p. 337; MS Rawl. A 41, p. 642)
Sir,
I Rite constantly to you, but I have no assurance, that you reseave them. I have reseaved but one from you since I left you. This day the Swead's imbassador is gone away: George Lis— went a littell before him. Our burgises for the parlament were chosen on weddensday last in the sety. We have chosen vere good religious men, such as major generall Browne, alderman Addams, alderman Foout, and Mr. Jones, six for the sety: but at Westminster ther was a very sad dispute, two men slane, and many wounded. It arose betwext one colonall Gravenar and one Mr. Latham. The solgers came in to cry for Gravenar, the setisans cried no sword men, no marsonary men; whereupon they fell together by the eares: the were like at Branford, where the Anabaptests tooke away the justises swords, and beate them meserablely, insomuch that a regement of horse was sent to part them. Theare is {d|a regement|7 74 30 54 29 50} of {d|hors|39 46 48} sent to lie nere {t|Plemoth|43 27 11 38 40 50 14} and six {t|companies|17 38 63 60 11 48} of {u|243} {d|men|226} are {d|sent|79 29 50} to strengthen {t|Hul|14 53 37} and {u|43 37 11 56 50 14 86 38} is so fare out of favour now, that {d|Thurlo|50 14 15 46 51} towlde me, he had a greattar mind to {u|33 39 13} him then {u|33 37 48 11}. Pray let me here from you, when I shall wate on you.
Your most faithfull servant,
Jo. Waddall.
The superscription, A mons. mons. Vanyeare marchant demeurant au logis de M. Robinson en Bruges, Flandres.

=== Letter 2. 29 August 1656. Signed Jo. Weddall, indorsed "Walters". (Birch vol. 5, pp. 364-365; MS Rawl. A 41, p. 842)
Sir,
I Very much desire to here from you, to know if you reseave myne: if I had reseaved ansors to my letters I have writt, I myte have bene forwarder in my busenesse; at presant I have an acquantance, who is able to instruckt me consarning the {t|Iland|58 89}, no man bettar; he is a {t|mastr|90 50 48 46} to it. He tells me there is thirty {d|sail|78 49} at Jemica; twelve {d|sail|18 49} at {t|Cales|13 27 11 48}, but them are devided, halfe of them are gone to {d|the Strats|50 14 11 18 50 46 17 50 48}; and hereupon the last of {u|210 211} and {u|212}. Here is {d|forti sail|26 46 50 58 78 49}; {u|48 54 81 39}, one who the {d|seamen|79 17 226} love extreamly had a garde set over {d|him|35 38} was fower dayes wating before he cowlde get to speake with the {d|Cownsel|16 52 29 79 27}, which he tooke very ill; {d|Ritch|75 50 19 14} is sent presonare to Winsor, and every day one or other {d|confined|16 29 25 59 11 10}. My frind's name is Mr. {u|52 15 48}, he is very kind with {u|48 54 81 39}, in so much that they call brothers. He hath promest me to labor all he can to bringe him to goodnesse; he is considerable. When I here further, you shall here from me consarning that. I have sent you this inclosed, to show you what honest men it hath pleased the cuntry to chuse {u|242}; I did except aganst sum of them that were chosen; we pray daly, that it may brede no defarance, yet it is much fered. {t|Tom|50 40 38} is strictly looked to; {d|Thurlo|50 14 15 46 51} hath promist me, that he shall be able to doe noe more aganst me than he hath allready, and that hereafter he will call him to account; he assures me, he allwayes tooke him for a knave. I would be with you, but hopps of getting them goods maks me stay as long as I may; you were pleased to promis me, that I shoulde here from you when it was seasonable to render you a vesit; tell then I expect to here what further sarvice lies in the power of
August the 29th, 1656.
Your most faithfull servant,
Jo. Weddall.
Indors'd, Walters.
